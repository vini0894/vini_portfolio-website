Case 01 · Cost Leakage

Finding the leak in a recommendation funnel at scale.

Scaling from one market to 36 changed the economics overnight. A proactive funnel review turned that shift into a structural cost cut, without touching the personalisation that drove satisfaction.

S$457K recovered annually, about 10% of operational cost, with no significant change to match rate or satisfaction.

01 · Problem

The system was tuned for a world that no longer existed.

The matching engine was built for a single market, where every provider cost about the same, so cost was never a variable. Then the platform grew to 36 countries and 700+ providers across dozens of rate tiers. Relevance-only ranking kept surfacing expensive providers in the exact slots that decide conversion.

75%of all selections happen in the top 5 results, the entire game
24%of users saw zero affordable options in their top 5
3.6×local rates, what some users were quietly matched against

02 · Method

Map the funnel first. Build second.

I reviewed the journey stage by stage to find where the economics broke, the Top 10 → Top 5 step, then tested three ways to fix it through simulation before recommending anything.

Additive weight

Add cost as a new scoring signal. Too weak to move rankings, and it diluted the signals already driving healthy matches.

Rejected

Hard bucketing

Always surface cheap providers first. Fixes cost, destroys personalisation. A great fit loses to a poor cheap one every time.

Shipped

Penalty-only multiplier

Penalise overpriced matches, never inflate cheap ones. Drops into the pipeline with zero changes upstream.

FinalScore = ExistingScore × CostScore

03 · Result

Cost as a guardrail on relevance, not a replacement for it.

Validated through a phased experiment with guardrails mapped to every funnel stage: two-tailed t-tests, 95% confidence, 5% minimum detectable effect.

04 · Decision

One page to leadership. A toolkit that outlasted the project.

I framed the three options as a one-page proposal in business language: what we'd gain, what we'd risk, why I recommended the conservative path. To ship it safely, I built a self-serve simulation toolkit so PMs could pressure-test the algorithm themselves, long after the project closed.

Efficiency as a constraint on personalisation, not a replacement for it.
