Case 04 · Data Infrastructure

The plumbing the whole team came to rely on.

Every question routed through an analyst. I standardised the data and built the tooling so product, growth, and ops could answer for themselves.

40% reduction in analysis overhead, on top of pipelines the whole org could finally trust.

01 · Problem

The analyst was the bottleneck.

Every "what happened" routed through a person. Event names were inconsistent, definitions drifted between teams, and answering a question took longer than deciding what to do with the answer.

02 · Method

Standardise the data. Then hand over the keys.

I defined a global event taxonomy with naming conventions and documentation, then built self-serve dashboards, conversion funnels, and journey flows on top of it across Mixpanel, Metabase, Looker Studio, and Tableau.

Alongside it I automated monthly payouts for 500+ providers with built-in bonus logic, and shipped a real-time alerting system to flag suspicious in-platform activity.

500+provider payouts automated each month
4BI tools unified on one event model
RTalerting for suspicious activity

03 · Result

Cleaner pipelines, faster answers, fewer requests.

Analysis overhead−40%

04 · Decision

Build the thing that outlasts the project.

The point was never one dashboard. It was a system where the team's velocity no longer depended on a single analyst being available, the kind of investment that keeps paying out long after the work is done.

The best analytics work makes the analyst optional.
