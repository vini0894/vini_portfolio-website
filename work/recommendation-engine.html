Case 02 · Recommendation Systems

A matching engine that learned what a good fit looks like.

Users were matched to coaches and counsellors by hand-written rules. I replaced them with a model trained on what actually led to good sessions.

80% lift in match quality over the rule-based baseline, with a 45% increase in match conversion.

01 · Problem

Rules don't scale, and they don't learn.

Matching ran on hand-tuned filters: language, timezone, availability. The logic was brittle, ignored what actually made a session land, and capped how good any match could get as supply grew across markets.

300+providers to score on every single request
36countries the system had to generalise across
0signal from what historically made a good match

02 · Method

Score on similarity. Then refine with structure.

I built a similarity-scoring engine trained on historical successful sessions, then layered post-processing for timezone proximity, language, availability, and preference attributes. Design philosophy: keep the signal set tight, so results stay relevant instead of over-constrained in a thin market.

Models in play: content-based filtering and logistic regression, validated against the existing rule-based matches before anything shipped.

03 · Result

Better matches, and more of them converting.

Match quality+80%
Match conversion+45%
EngagementHigher

04 · Decision

This wasn't a feature. It was the conversion mechanism.

Once it shipped, 75% of all selections came from the top 5 results the engine produced. That made it the single highest-leverage surface on the platform, and the natural place to later layer cost efficiency without touching relevance.

A recommendation engine isn't a feature. It's the conversion mechanism.
